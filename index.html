I am a Ph.D. candidate in Physics at the University of California, Los Angeles (UCLA), working in the Quantum Matter Design Studio (QMDS) with Dr. Christopher Gutiérrez. I use scanning tunneling microscopy (STM) and angle-resolved photoemission spectroscopy (ARPES) to image — and control — how electrons organize themselves in low-dimensional quantum materials. My thesis is on imaging and controlling microscopic charge density waves, using in situ tuning knobs such as in-plane electric fields and strain.

Before UCLA, I received my B.S. in Physics from National Taiwan University. Along the way I've come to enjoy the engineering side of experiments as much as the physics: ultra-high vacuum, cryogenics, and Python pipelines for instrument control and image analysis. Off the clock I play table tennis, boulder, and hike.
